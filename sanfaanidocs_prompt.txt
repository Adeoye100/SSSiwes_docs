SANFAANI Project Context and Handoff Document
1. Project Overview

SANFAANI is a business operations and service-management platform developed during the user's SIWES experience. The project was created to apply software engineering knowledge to a practical operational problem rather than building only an academic demonstration.

The central idea is to bring several everyday business activities into one connected system. These activities include device charging, workspace usage, inventory, sales, payments, receipts, customer collection, staff operations and administrative records.

Before a system like this, these activities can easily become fragmented across manual records, separate processes or disconnected tools. SANFAANI provides a central application for recording activities, managing resources and maintaining traceable operational records.

The project therefore demonstrates the transition from learning software engineering concepts to applying those concepts to a real operational environment.

2. Problem the Project Addresses

The main problem SANFAANI addresses is operational fragmentation.

A business that provides services such as device charging and workspace access also needs to manage:

customer registrations
charging sessions
available charging capacity
workspace usage
inventory
sales
payments
receipts
customer claims
staff activities
financial records
historical records

When these activities are handled independently, it becomes more difficult to maintain accurate records and understand the current state of the business.

SANFAANI was designed to bring these activities together into a single operational system.

The objective is not simply to digitize individual tasks. It is to create a connected flow where an operational action produces the necessary business records automatically.

For example:

Customer service → transaction → receipt → record → operational history

This gives the business a clearer and more traceable representation of what has happened.

5. Core Application Modules

SANFAANI is organized around several connected operational areas.

Charging Management

The charging module manages device charging activities.

It allows the system to keep track of:

charging sessions
occupied and available capacity
customer/device information
charging status
collection status

A charging session is connected to the relevant transaction and receipt.

The system also uses a secure claim mechanism for device collection.

Workspace Management

The workspace functionality allows the business to record and manage workspace usage.

This gives the business a way to track:

workspace registration
customer usage
available capacity
associated operational records

The workspace module is part of the wider operational system rather than functioning as a completely separate application.

Inventory Management

The inventory module manages stock and stock movement.

The system tracks information such as:

stock quantity
cost price
selling price
stock movements
inventory value

SANFAANI distinguishes between:

Stock Units

The number of physical units available.

Inventory Cost

The value of the current stock based on its acquisition cost.

Retail Value

The potential value of the stock when sold.

Potential Margin

The difference between potential retail value and inventory cost.

These values are deliberately kept separate from actual revenue or profit.

Sales

The sales component records product/service transactions and connects them with the broader financial record of the application.

This allows operational transactions to contribute to a centralized history rather than existing as isolated records.

Receipts

Receipts provide customers with evidence of their transactions.

SANFAANI supports receipt generation and PDF documents.

For charging transactions, the receipt can also contain a secure QR-based claim mechanism.

Financial Records

Transactions are recorded centrally so that operational activities can be traced back to their financial effect.

This provides the foundation for:

transaction history
financial records
operational reporting
reconciliation

The intention is to avoid treating payment records as disconnected from the activity that generated them.

Notifications

The application supports notification-related functionality for operational events.

This provides a mechanism for communicating relevant information to users without making notifications part of the core transaction itself.

6. Customer Receipt and Charging Flow

One of the most important workflows in SANFAANI is the charging process.

The simplified flow is:

Customer requests charging
        ↓
Staff creates charging session
        ↓
Transaction is recorded
        ↓
Receipt is generated
        ↓
Secure claim information is created
        ↓
Device is charged
        ↓
Customer returns
        ↓
Staff scans/verifies claim
        ↓
System confirms valid collection
        ↓
Device is released
        ↓
Charging session is completed

The claim mechanism is particularly important because the receipt is not merely a payment document. For charging services, it also becomes part of the device collection process.

The QR claim uses a secure one-time token.

The system verifies the claim before allowing collection, helping prevent the same claim from being reused.

7. Receipt Delivery

SANFAANI also has an intended WhatsApp receipt delivery capability.

The important architectural decision is that WhatsApp delivery is not part of the core financial transaction.

The business transaction is completed first.

Then receipt delivery can happen separately.

Conceptually:

Transaction
    ↓
Database confirmation
    ↓
Receipt generated
    ↓
WhatsApp delivery

If WhatsApp is unavailable or fails, the completed transaction remains valid.

This separation prevents an external communication service from causing a completed business transaction to fail.

WhatsApp delivery is also designed around explicit customer consent rather than assuming that possessing a phone number automatically gives permission to send messages.

The WhatsApp integration remains dependent on the appropriate provider credentials, approved template and configuration.

8. Application Architecture

SANFAANI follows a layered architecture.

USER
  ↓
FRONTEND
  ↓
API
  ↓
AUTHENTICATION / AUTHORIZATION
  ↓
BUSINESS LOGIC
  ↓
DATABASE

The frontend is responsible for presenting the application and allowing staff or administrators to interact with it.

The backend is responsible for:

receiving requests
authenticating users
enforcing permissions
validating information
executing business rules
communicating with the database
producing responses

The database stores the persistent business information.

The frontend does not directly manage the database.

This separation makes the system easier to maintain and provides an important security boundary.

12. Authentication and Authorization

SANFAANI uses Supabase Authentication for user identity and session management.

The basic flow is:

User signs in
     ↓
Supabase authenticates user
     ↓
Frontend receives session
     ↓
Access token accompanies API requests
     ↓
Backend verifies identity
     ↓
Backend checks application role
     ↓
Request is accepted or rejected

There is an important distinction between authentication and authorization.

Authentication answers:

“Who is this user?”

Authorization answers:

“What is this user allowed to do?”

SANFAANI uses the backend to enforce the second part.

This means simply having a valid login does not automatically give a user administrative privileges.

13. API Communication

The frontend and backend communicate through an API contract.

SANFAANI uses an OpenAPI-based approach and a generated API client.

This gives the frontend and backend a shared understanding of:

available endpoints
request structures
response structures
expected data types

The generated client reduces the need to manually duplicate API definitions across the frontend.

The result is a clearer boundary between the user interface and server-side operations.

16. Security Approach

Security was treated as part of the application's architecture rather than as something added at the end.

Important areas include:

Authentication

Users must have a valid authenticated session.

Role-based authorization

Different roles receive different permissions.

Backend enforcement

Security rules are enforced on the server rather than relying only on frontend visibility.

Input validation

Incoming data is validated before business operations are performed.

Rate limiting

The backend has rate-limiting protections around sensitive operations.

Security headers

The backend uses security middleware such as Helmet.

Audit records

Important operational activity can be traced through audit information.

Secure claims

Charging collection uses a secure claim token rather than relying only on a visible receipt number.

Secret separation

Backend credentials such as service-role credentials are not intended to be exposed through frontend environment variables.

17. Reliability and Transaction Design

One of the important engineering lessons from SANFAANI is that a business transaction can involve multiple systems.

For example:

Customer payment
      ↓
Database
      ↓
Receipt
      ↓
WhatsApp

The database operation and external communication should not be treated as one inseparable action.

SANFAANI therefore separates the core business transaction from secondary services such as WhatsApp delivery.

This protects the integrity of the primary record.

The same principle applies to other operations where consistency matters. A completed financial operation should not disappear simply because an external notification failed.

18. Inventory Business Logic

The inventory system deliberately distinguishes between different kinds of value.

For example:

Stock Units
    ↓
How many items exist?

Inventory Cost
    ↓
What did the current stock cost?

Retail Value
    ↓
What could the current stock sell for?

Potential Margin
    ↓
Retail Value - Inventory Cost

This distinction is important because potential retail value is not the same thing as actual revenue, and potential margin is not automatically realized profit.

This makes the dashboard more meaningful for operational management.

26. One-Paragraph Project Summary

SANFAANI is a full-stack business operations platform developed during SIWES to solve the problem of fragmented day-to-day business management. The system brings services such as device charging, workspace usage, inventory, sales, transactions and receipts into one connected application. It provides role-based access for administrators and staff, while customer-facing outputs such as receipts and charging collection claims are generated from the same operational records. The application uses a React and Vite frontend, an Express and TypeScript backend, Supabase for authentication, MongoDB for data storage and supporting services for receipts, QR claims, notifications and communication. Beyond implementing features, the project focused on security, data consistency, API communication, testing and deployment, making it a practical application of software engineering knowledge to a real operational problem.
